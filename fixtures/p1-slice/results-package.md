## 1. Study summary

- Design: two-condition, between-subject experiment with randomized assignment.
- Participants: n = 40 healthy adults (20 per condition), ages 18–35, single site.
- Conditions: **A** = normal overnight sleep (≥ 7 h, verified by actigraphy); **B** = sleep-restricted night (≤ 4 h, verified).
- Task: 16-item word-pair cued recall, administered 30 minutes after waking. **Immediate recall only.**

## 2. Reported results (as supplied by the Principal)

| Metric | A — normal sleep | B — sleep restricted |
|---|---|---|
| Mean items recalled (of 16) | 14.1 (SD 2.9) | 12.4 (SD 3.2) |
| Median response time per pair | 2.9 s | 3.1 s |

Statistics as supplied: Welch's *t*(38) = 2.31, *p* = 0.026, Cohen's *d* = 0.73 for the recall comparison; response-time comparison not significant (*p* = 0.41).

## 3. Procedure (as reported)

Paired-associate learning: encoding session in the evening, sleep manipulation overnight, cued recall test the next morning. Standard exclusion criteria (self-reported sleep, neurological, or psychiatric conditions) were applied.

## 4. User-stated findings (verbatim)

- **F1:** "Participants with normal sleep recalled more word pairs than sleep-restricted participants (14.1 vs 12.4; *p* = 0.026)."
- **F2:** "Sleep deprivation impairs long-term memory consolidation, reducing retention by up to 15%."
- **F3:** "Retention after seven days was significantly better in the normal-sleep group." (Follow-up data have been collected but are **not yet entered or verified**; no numbers are supplied.)

## 5. Limitations (as stated by the Principal)

Small sample; single site; healthy volunteers; a single recall measure; follow-up data not yet verified.
